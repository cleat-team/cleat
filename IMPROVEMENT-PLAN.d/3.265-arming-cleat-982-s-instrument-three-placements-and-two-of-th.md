### 3.265 Arming cleat#982's instrument: three placements, and two of them decide nothing — ⬜ ARMED, cleat#982 still open

3.264 landed the instrument that tells a deleted row from an invisible one, and left it wired to
nothing but its own self-test. This arms it on every SQL Server backend test. It fixes no defect;
what it removes is the reason cleat#982's next occurrence would again be unreadable after the fact.

**Three placements were tried. Two report the same thing whatever is true**, which is the
"condition that never decides anything" case in CLAUDE.md — the hardest kind to notice, because
every run looks like a working instrument.

Measured 2026-09-16 on a database built from the shipped migrations. Every case seeds first and
refuses to report if the seed is not visible to a reader that should see it; the first two drafts
of the probe printed `SEED FAILED ... UNMEASURED` on a column-name mistake rather than a tidy pair
of zeros, which is the only reason the numbers below are worth anything.

| `readerDB` | reading | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| the raw `testutil.TestDB` handle (`sa`, plain pool) | `physical=1 visible=0` | PRESENT BUT INVISIBLE — **always** |
| `MSSQLAdminDB`'s pool | `physical=1 visible=1` | consistent — **always** |
| the tenant store, one tenant | `physical=3 visible=3` | consistent |
| the tenant store, **two** tenants | `physical=5 visible=3` | PRESENT BUT INVISIBLE |

Row 1 is the placement that mirrors `ReportForeignSessionsOnFailure`, and it is the one anybody
would reach for. It cannot work: since migration 075 the shipped `fn_tenant_filter` is the plain
form and names no role, so `sa` has no exemption and a plain pool with no session context sees
nothing in a policy-bearing table — on a healthy database as readily as on a broken one. Measured
in **both** predicate forms, because `MSSQLAdminDB` applies the cross-tenant opt-in and so any run
that has called `CleanupMSSQLTestData` once is no longer pristine; `plain` and `admin` both read
`physical=1 visible=0`.

Row 2 fails in the opposite direction and is the more dangerous of the two, because the constant it
prints is the reassuring one.

Rows 3 and 4 differ, so the tenant store can decide — and it is also the handle the failing reads
in cleat#982 actually went through. Row 4 is its false positive: **`physical` counts every tenant's
rows and a tenant pool counts one tenant's**, so two rows belonging to somebody else read as a
disappearance. That is inherent rather than a bug — being table-level index metadata is exactly why
no predicate is evaluated against `sys.dm_db_partition_stats`, and it is why the reading cannot be
scoped to a tenant.

So the verdict moves to a count over **one population**: `COUNT(*) WHERE tenant_id = @p1` through a
cross-tenant reader, against what the tenant's own pool can see. `physical` stays in the report as
context, labelled as expected whenever another tenant has rows.

**And the placement is not `t.Cleanup`, which is the part that had to be measured rather than
reasoned about.** Two orderings defeat it, and the second survives the fix for the first:

| placement | what defeats it | reading |
|---|---|---|
| `t.Cleanup` in `Setup` | the test's `defer teardown()` closes `db` first | `UNMEASURED: "database is closed"` |
| `t.Cleanup`, pools kept open | teardown's `CleanupMSSQLTestData` wipes the table first | `truth=0 visible=0 -- consistent` |

A deferred call in the test body runs before any `t.Cleanup` callback, and every backend test here
is written `defer teardown()`. The second row was measured with the pools deliberately left open so
"closed" could not be the explanation: the **same readers, over the same run**, read
`truth=5 visible=0 PRESENT BUT INVISIBLE` at failure time and `truth=0 visible=0 consistent` from a
`t.Cleanup` a moment later. Nothing reports an error in between. The report is therefore called at
the **top of teardown**, before the blanket DELETE.

Both of those were found by a known-positive — a forced failure through `MSSQLBackend.Setup` with
rows present — and neither would have been found by the suite going green, which it did throughout.

`TestTheRowDisappearanceVerdictIsNotConstant` is the regression, and it asserts on the **text** of
three verdicts rather than on a status: a healthy single-tenant store, another tenant holding rows,
and a reader that has lost its tenant context. Falsified by reverting the verdict to the table-wide
comparison, which goes red on exactly the two false positives the change removes.

**Still open on cleat#982:** runs 1-3. This makes their next occurrence readable; it does not
explain the ones already observed, which remains unknowable after the fact.
