- **A worker at its concurrency limit looked for work again only at the next `--poll` tick, even when a slot was freed a moment after it began waiting (cleat#4311).** The dispatch loop slept a whole `--poll` when it had no free slot, and a plain sleep cannot be woken, so every freed slot sat unused until the tick. It now waits for a freed slot, the worker's shutdown, or the tick, whichever comes first. Measured 2026-11-25 on PostgreSQL 16 with real workers on one Apple M4, `--poll 500ms`, interleaved before and after: a signal delivered 150 ms after a run suspended resumed in 355 to 412 ms at `--concurrency 1` before the change and 92 to 110 ms after (30 trials each, first trial of each run discarded); and a worker at `--concurrency 2` with 40 short queued runs took about 9.9 s before and about 0.8 s after, with residency off and at the default. That second figure is a minimal guest whose runs last tens of milliseconds, so every run's end met a tick; runs that last longer than a tick lose proportionally less, and it is not a claim about production throughput. A worker with a free slot, and a worker at `--concurrency 4` or more with idle slots, measured the same before and after. A NOTIFY while the worker has no free slot is still not acted on until a slot frees, by design: it would find nothing to claim.
