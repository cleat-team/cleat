section: upgrade
---
- **How many runs a worker holds is now a share of its memory, not 8 (cleat#4377).** `--resident-memory-percent` (default **20**) is the share of the worker's memory limit that held runs may be charged, computed once at start-up and logged with where the figure came from: a cgroup v2 `memory.max`, a cgroup v1 limit, or, with **a warning**, the host's physical memory when there is no limit (on a host shared with other workloads that can be most of what they need: set a container limit or lower the percentage). **`--resident-max-runs` now defaults to 1024 and is a ceiling on the count, where it was 8 and was the limit;** the budget normally binds first (on a 2 GiB limit, 410 MiB is about 17 Go runs at the 24 MiB charge; on the Helm chart's current 512Mi limit, 102 MiB is 4 Go runs, 6 Python or 1 C#), so a worker may hold MORE runs than it did where memory allows and fewer where it does not. **`--resident-memory-budget-mb` above 0 still overrides the percentage**, and an install that set `--resident-max-runs` explicitly keeps that count as its ceiling. **`--resident-memory-percent 0` turns residency off**, like `--resident-max-runs 0`. If the worker's memory cannot be read at all, the budget is 8 runs of the dearest language and a warning says so. The charge per held run is still an estimate by language (Go 24 MiB, Python 17 MiB, C# 66 MiB, not lowered here), a full budget refuses the wait with reason `memory` and it suspends as before, and nothing is evicted to make room. Two gauges, `cleat_resident_memory_budget_bytes` and `cleat_resident_memory_charged_bytes`, show the budget and what is charged.
