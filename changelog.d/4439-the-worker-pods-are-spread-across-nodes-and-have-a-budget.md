- **The Helm chart and the raw `k8s/` manifests now ask the scheduler to keep worker pods on different nodes, and add a PodDisruptionBudget (cleat#4439).** The chart set no affinity and had no budget, so nothing kept the autoscaler's floor of two workers on separate hardware except the scheduler's own spreading, and a node drain could evict both at once. The Deployment now carries a `topologySpreadConstraint` on `kubernetes.io/hostname` with `maxSkew: 1` and `whenUnsatisfiable: ScheduleAnyway` (`topologySpread` in `values.yaml`), and a `PodDisruptionBudget` with `maxUnavailable: 1` is rendered (`podDisruptionBudget`; `k8s/pdb.yaml` for the raw manifests). **The default spreads but does not refuse**: on a cluster with one schedulable node, or when the other node is full or cordoned, the workers still start together. Set `topologySpread.whenUnsatisfiable=DoNotSchedule` to have a pod stay Pending instead; `topologySpread.zoneKey` adds a zone constraint; `topologySpread.enabled=false` and `podDisruptionBudget.enabled=false` turn either off; `affinity` is still yours to set. Measured on kind (2026-11-23), one of two nodes cordoned: the default started both workers on the one node and strict left the second Pending; the budget refused the second of two back-to-back evictions and, with it off, both were accepted. On two healthy nodes the scheduler separated the workers with the constraint off as well (15 of 15 installs), so on a healthy cluster this makes the requirement explicit rather than changing where pods land. See `docs/operations/zero-downtime-deploy.md`.
