- **A worker compiles ONE component at a time, and counts the compiles a failed `--component-precompile` child sent back to it (cleat#4397).** Before, a burst of runs of several DISTINCT Python components compiled all of them at once in the worker (the per-component lock only made runs of the SAME component wait): measured in a 2 GiB container, six first compiles at once took the container from 40 MiB to its limit in 17 seconds and it was killed by the out-of-memory killer, with half of the runs never started. Now they queue, in the child or in the worker, and the same burst starts and finishes all of its runs; the log says when a compile waited. **This removes the simultaneous peak and not the retention:** a compile in the worker keeps for good 198 to 308 MiB for each of the first four further distinct Python components and 44 to 76 for each of the next three (mean 175), so a worker without a precompile child still holds about six distinct Python components at 2 GiB, and the seventh sat at the limit in one run and killed the container in another. A child that fails for an ordinary reason (not killed by the out-of-memory killer) falls back to compiling in the worker; that is now counted on `cleat_component_precompile_fallbacks_total{reason}` (`timeout`, `child_exit`, `record`, `artifact`, `deserialize`, `workdir`, `other`) and the WARN says where to look, because a child that fails systematically turns the flag into compiling in the worker with no other sign. Single runs on one arm64 machine; x86-64 is not measured.
