- **A worker now holds runs on itself through a short wait by default (cleat#4162); 0.5.0 held none.** A Go, Python or C# run that sleeps, or waits on `await_signals`, for a time inside the window (about 10 s at the defaults: the larger of 10 times the estimated replay time and a 10 s floor, capped at 5 min) is held in memory and woken in place, instead of being suspended and replayed. **At the defaults only short waits are held; long approval waits are not, by design.** The memory this takes is bounded: held runs may be charged **20% of the worker's memory** (`--resident-memory-percent`, or `--resident-memory-budget-mb` above 0, which overrides it), `--resident-max-runs` (default **1024**) is a ceiling on the count, and `--memory-soft-limit` is a second guard; how the budget is derived and what each run is charged is in the cleat#4377 entries. **A worker that dies while it holds runs delays their wakes until they are reclaimed** (40 to 41 s against 9.2 to 9.5 s with residency off in the kill-while-held scenario, measured on one loaded machine; every effect still happens once, and the production reclaim window comes on top: about 47 s at the default `--heartbeat`, derived from its formula and not measured; a faster reclaim for held runs is cleat#4275, 0.7.0). **To turn it off, set `--resident-max-runs 0` (or `--resident-memory-percent 0`).** The ceiling and the percentage are starting points, and tuning them on real workflows is cleat#4187. See `docs/reference/worker-config.md`.
