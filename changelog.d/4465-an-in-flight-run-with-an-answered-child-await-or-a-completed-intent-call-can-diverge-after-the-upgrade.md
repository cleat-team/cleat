---
section: upgrade
---
- **A run in flight across this upgrade can diverge at a call whose request used the durable clock after an answered child await or a completed write-ahead-intent call; let in-flight runs finish before upgrading (cleat#4465).** The fix stores the answer's time for every answer written from now on. It cannot repair a row written before it: the row holds one time, and for an `await_child` or `await_all_children` that was answered over a pending record, or a write-ahead-intent call completed over its intent row, that time is when the await or the call STARTED, with nothing in the row that says when it was answered. So a run whose history already holds such a record, and which uses the durable clock (`Now()`) in a later step, replays a clock earlier than the one it recorded with, and the replay can differ from the live run. The ways measured: the clock in the request body of a later call (the request comparison, cleat#4424, refuses it with `replay divergence at step N: ... with a different request`); the clock choosing WHICH call is made (the replay makes the other one: `call S.O, recorded S.P`); and the clock deciding WHETHER a call is made, with a constant request (the replay does not make the recorded call, and reports no divergence at that point). This is not a list of the cases that are affected: any other use of the clock after such a record can differ too, and not always as a refusal. Before upgrading, let those runs finish, or terminate and restart them; there is nothing to do for a run that starts after the upgrade. Every history written before this change carries the old timestamps, whatever build wrote it. A history that was already compacted keeps the timestamps compaction copied, so it keeps them after the upgrade too. **Embedders that do not pass `WithWorkflowStartTime`** (the worker does) seed a replay's clock from the first record's timestamp: when a run's FIRST step is an answered child await or a completed intent call, a `Now()` read before that step now reads later on a replay than it did live, by about how long the step was pending (measured at +131 to +148 ms with two 60 ms waits, against +0 to +2 ms before the change). The worker passes `WithWorkflowStartTime`, so worker deployments are unaffected.
