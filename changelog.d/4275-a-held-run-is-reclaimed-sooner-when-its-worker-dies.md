- **A run held in memory through a wait is reclaimed at `--held-reclaim-after` (about 17 s at the default heartbeat) instead of `--reclaim-timeout` (about 47 s) when its worker dies (cleat#4275).** Measured on PostgreSQL at the defaults with a real `SIGKILL` and no lease ageing, a held run's next step ran 49.1 to 49.9 s after the kill (three runs on develop and three on the 0.6.0 release tip); the chaos case `TestChaosSigkillWhileAHeldRunSleepsIsReclaimedAtTheHeldWindowWithoutAgeingTheLease` ran it 19.7, 21.0 and 20.0 s after the kill with the fast path (three runs, 1 s heartbeat, the reaper of a live worker), and 49.7 s with the held arm removed from `ReapStaleInstances` (one run). The window is the reclaim window without its database-stall allowance, because a held run is not executing and its wake is guarded by a fenced claim check, so a held run reclaimed by mistake costs one replay and not a repeated effect. The reaper still waits the full `--reclaim-timeout` after any database trouble of its own. `--held-reclaim-after` takes a duration, 0 derives it, a negative value turns the fast path off; it is refused below its derived floor. A worker marks a run held when a hold starts and clears the marker, fenced on its claim, before the hold's claim check returns and the guest resumes.
