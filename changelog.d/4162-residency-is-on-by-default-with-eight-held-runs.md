section: upgrade
---
- **A worker now holds runs on itself through a short wait by default (cleat#4162): `--resident-max-runs` defaults to 8, where it was 0 (off).** A Go, Python or C# run that sleeps, or waits on `await_signals`, for a time inside the window (about 10 s at the defaults: the larger of 10 times the estimated replay time and a 10 s floor, capped at 5 min) is held in memory and woken in place, instead of being suspended and replayed. **At the defaults only short waits are held; long approval waits are not, by design.** Memory is bounded by `--resident-memory-budget-mb`, which at its default of 0 means `--resident-max-runs` times the dearest language (C# at 66 MiB, so 528 MiB at 8 runs): a derived bound, not unlimited, and `--memory-soft-limit` is a second guard. **A worker that dies while it holds runs delays their wakes until they are reclaimed** (40 to 41 s against 9.2 to 9.5 s with residency off in the kill-while-held scenario, measured on one loaded machine; every effect still happens once, and the production reclaim window comes on top: about 47 s at the default `--heartbeat`, derived from its formula and not measured; a faster reclaim for held runs is cleat#4275, 0.7.0). **To restore the previous behaviour, set `--resident-max-runs 0`.** The count of 8 is a conservative guess and tuning it on real workflows is cleat#4187. See `docs/reference/worker-config.md`.
